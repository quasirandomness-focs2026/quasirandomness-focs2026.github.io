Algorithmic Perspectives on Quasirandomness

FOCS 2026 Workshop

Date and Time: Sunday, November 8 from 12:30 pm to 3:00 pm

Organizers: Cassandra Marcussen, Ted Pyne, Ronitt Rubinfeld, Asaf Shapira

Overview

Random graphs are both useful and broadly studied within theoretical computer science, in large part because they possess a broad array of structural properties with high probability, including a large spectral gap, the correct number of copies of every fixed subgraph, and uniformly spread edges.

Remarkably, many of these deterministic properties are essentially equivalent. The seminal work of Chung, Graham, and Wilson (Combinatorica 1989) established the equivalence of a broad collection of random-like properties, including the strikingly simple condition of having the correct number of 4-cycles. Graphs satisfying these conditions are called "quasirandom graphs."

This workshop is motivated by the algorithmic principle that structure possessed by random instances can often be certified using a small collection of deterministic statistics, such as degrees, codegrees, or small subgraph counts. Such certificates can help algorithms recognize tractable average-case instances, distinguish random graph models, infer global structure from local observations, and translate counting estimates into complexity consequences. Recent results have developed this principle independently across several areas of theoretical computer science, but there is not yet a unifying framework explaining which quasirandom conditions are most useful for which computational tasks.

The workshop will introduce and explore modern algorithmic perspectives on quasirandomness, with the goal of understanding how recent definitions, techniques, and applications relate to one another.
